# Evaluation of Internal Documentation on Employee Resource Provisioning Procedures

## Executive Summary

This research briefing presents the findings of an internal information audit conducted to evaluate the established workflows, tools, and protocols for provisioning employee resources. The scope of this inquiry spanned digital resource allocation, hardware and physical asset distribution, operational workflows, and security/permission configurations. 

The investigation revealed a systemic absence of documented procedures across all investigated domains within the evaluated internal repositories. There are currently no records, policies, or standardized guidelines detailing how physical or digital assets are requested, allocated, managed, or secured for personnel. This complete lack of documentation represents a critical operational vulnerability and highlights an immediate need for the establishment and formalization of structured employee onboarding and resource provisioning protocols.

## Key Takeaways

* **Absence of Digital Provisioning Protocols:** There are currently no documented standard operating procedures (SOPs) for provisioning digital resources, IT accounts, or software licenses.
* **Lack of Asset Allocation Frameworks:** No internal documentation exists to define how physical hardware, office equipment, or other tangible assets are requested, approved, or distributed to employees.
* **Unidentified Tooling and Workflows:** The software platforms, tracking systems, and operational workflows used to manage the lifecycle of employee resources are completely undocumented.
* **Undefined Security and Role-Based Access Controls:** There is no documented framework or governance policy detailing how security permissions, roles, and access levels are configured during the resource setup process.

## Detailed Findings

### Provisioning of Digital Resources, IT Accounts, and Software Licenses

An analysis of internal repositories was conducted to determine the standard procedures for provisioning digital resources, setting up IT accounts, and assigning software licenses to employees. The investigation yielded no documented information, procedures, or guidelines addressing these tasks. There are no records indicating which departments are responsible for initializing these accounts, what baseline software suites are required by default, or how software licenses are procured and managed during the employee onboarding phase.

### Request and Allocation of Physical Resources, Hardware, and Office Equipment

A review of the documentation regarding the requisition and allocation of physical resources—including laptops, mobile devices, monitors, and office furniture—was performed. No information was found in the internal documents to outline this process. There is a complete lack of documented policies governing procurement thresholds, approval workflows, inventory tracking, or asset deployment mechanisms for physical equipment.

### Workflows, Tools, and Platforms for Resource Tracking

The inquiry sought to identify the specific ticketing systems, IT Service Management (ITSM) platforms, or human resource information systems (HRIS) utilized to request, track, and audit the creation and assignment of employee resources. No information found in the internal documents references any workflows, tools, or platforms dedicated to these functions. Consequently, the operational mechanisms for lifecycle management of both physical and digital assets remain undocumented.

### Configuration of Roles, Permissions, and Security Access Levels

Establishing appropriate security postures requires clearly defined protocols for configuring roles, permissions, and security access levels during the employee setup process. The audit sought to identify these authorization standards. No information was found in the internal documents regarding how security profiles are determined, how Principle of Least Privilege (PoLP) is enforced, or who is authorized to approve elevated access permissions for new or transitioning employees.
